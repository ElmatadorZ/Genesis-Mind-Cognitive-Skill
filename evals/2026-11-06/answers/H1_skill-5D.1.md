**Short answer:** Probably yes, but only after you renegotiate the terms. As written, you'd be giving them more than 40% of your revenue is worth. The money isn't the danger. The exclusivity clause is, together with what happens in month 13.

---

📍 **SITUATION ANALYSIS**

**Known:** 5 people, a 12-month retainer, about 40% of revenue, and exclusivity within the client's industry.
**Inferred:** You aren't currently fully booked (otherwise 40% of new revenue would need new hires). Some of your current or future pipeline is in that same industry.
**Unknown (and these decide it):**
- How much of your revenue and pipeline is in that industry today
- Your margin on retainer work compared with project work
- How the exclusivity is defined: the industry, a list of named competitors, or "anything adjacent"
- How long it lasts: just the 12 months, or a tail after the contract ends
- The client's termination rights and notice period

Is "40% of revenue" an addition or a share? If it's on top of what you have now, you nearly double in size. If it replaces existing work, it becomes 40% of your business. These are very different deals.

---

📍 **FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN**

**Assumption 1:** "12 months of guaranteed revenue = security."
**Challenge:** Most retainers can be ended on 30–90 days' notice. If this one can, it's really a 1–3 month guarantee with a 12-month restriction attached.
**Truth:** The real guarantee equals the notice period. The real cost equals how long the exclusivity lasts.

**Assumption 2:** "Exclusivity only costs the clients we turn away."
**Challenge:** It also costs pipeline you never see. Referrals stop, case studies can't be shown, and your positioning in that industry shrinks to "that client's agency."
**Truth:** Exclusivity is a price. You are selling an option on your capacity and your market. That option has a value and should be paid for.

**Assumption 3:** "Concentration risk is about the money."
**Challenge:** At 5 people it's also about operations. One client's priorities, culture and approval cycles start to shape how the whole studio works.
**Truth:** Above roughly 25–30% from one client, you are in practice part-owned by them. That's a common rule of thumb for agencies, not a law.

---

📍 **SYSTEM MAP**

```
Retainer income → stable cash → less sales effort → thinner pipeline
      ↑                                                  ↓
Client leverage ← higher dependency ← fewer alternatives at renewal
```
**Reinforcing loop:** the longer you stay, the weaker your bargaining position at renewal. The client knows this.
**Damping loop:** strong delivery builds case studies and a reputation, but only if you're allowed to publish them.
**Leverage points:** the exclusivity definition, the termination notice, and a no-tail clause. Small edits to these change the deal's risk a lot.

---

📍 **MULTI-AGENT INSIGHT**

**Analyst:** You can't value this without your current share of revenue from that industry. If it's 0–5%, exclusivity is nearly free. If it's 20%+, you are giving up existing income to gain new income.

**Strategist:** Exclusivity has real value to the client: they're paying to keep you away from competitors. That's leverage you have. Ask for a 15–30% premium over your normal rate, or a minimum guaranteed spend. If they won't pay anything for it, they may be valuing it lower than they claim.

**Skeptic:** "40% of revenue" sounds big because you're small. The real question is whether they're buying your work or buying cheap, locked-in capacity. Watch for scope creep: retainers without defined deliverables tend to expand until margin disappears. And ask why a client this size wants exclusivity from a 5-person studio. Is there a competitor they're specifically blocking?

**Forecaster:** See scenarios below.

**Executor:** Don't answer yes or no. Answer with a counter-proposal (see Recommendation).

---

📍 **SCENARIOS** (rough weightings, based only on the facts given)

🐂 **Bull (~25%):** Good margin, narrow exclusivity, smooth relationship. You use the stable cash to build a second vertical and renew on better terms.

⚖️ **Base (~45%):** It works, but scope grows, sales slow down, and at month 12 you renew under pressure because replacing 40% of revenue in a quarter is hard.

🐻 **Bear (~25%):** The client's internal sponsor leaves, budgets get cut, or they bring design in-house. They terminate with short notice. You're left with a gap in revenue, a thin pipeline, and possibly a tail clause still blocking the industry you know best.

💀 **Black Swan (<5%):** The client is acquired or merges, and their IP/exclusivity terms carry over to a much larger parent. Or a dispute leaves you without payment while you're still bound by exclusivity.

---

📍 **DECISION OPTIONS**

**Option A: Accept as offered**
Trade-off: Fastest, most cash certainty. Risk: you carry all the downside (termination, tail, scope) without being paid for it. Breaks if the notice period is short or exclusivity is broad.

**Option B: Accept with renegotiated terms** ← recommended path
Trade-off: Some risk the client walks away, and a slower close. Risk: low, as long as you negotiate in good faith. Breaks if the client treats any pushback as a dealbreaker, which tells you something useful about them too.

**Option C: Decline exclusivity, offer a non-exclusive retainer**
Trade-off: Keeps your market open, but may lose the deal or bring a lower fee. Best if that industry is already a big part of your revenue or pipeline.

**Option D: Decline**
Only rational if that industry is your core market and they won't narrow the clause.

---

📍 **FINAL RECOMMENDATION**

Go for **Option B**. Counter with:

1. **Narrow exclusivity** to a named list of direct competitors (e.g. 3–5 companies), not "the industry."
2. **No tail.** Exclusivity ends the day the retainer ends, or the day they terminate, whichever comes first.
3. **Minimum commitment:** a 6-month non-cancellable period or 90-day notice, so the guarantee lasts as long as the restriction.
4. **An exclusivity premium**, or a guaranteed minimum monthly fee regardless of usage.
5. **Defined scope:** monthly hours or deliverables, with overage billed separately.
6. **Portfolio rights:** the right to show the work, even if delayed or anonymized.

Whatever happens, treat month 1 as the start of replacing them. Keep at least one person spending part of their time on new business so no single client goes above roughly 40% for good.

**This recommendation is invalid if:**
- That industry already makes up a large share of your revenue or pipeline → lean toward C
- The client refuses any notice period or minimum commitment → lean toward C or D
- You're currently in a cash crisis with less than ~3 months of runway → Option A may be justified for survival, with eyes open

**CONFIDENCE:** ~60% in Option B as the right direction. The actual terms aren't known yet.
**UNKNOWNS:** current industry share of revenue, retainer margin, termination clause, exclusivity definition and tail, your cash runway.
**WHAT CHANGES THIS:** any of the unknowns above, plus how the client reacts to the counter-proposal. That reaction is the best information you'll get about the next 12 months.

*Contract terms like exclusivity, tail and termination clauses are worth a short review by a lawyer before signing.*
