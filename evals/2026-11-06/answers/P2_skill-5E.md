📍 **SITUATION**

You've given me three facts: two options (expand into Vietnam next year, or double down on Thailand), 14 months of runway, and a planning horizon of "next year". You haven't told me your product, revenue, growth rate, burn, or how much traction you have in Thailand. So everything below depends on those unknowns, and I'll point out where each one flips the answer.

📍 **FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN**

Assumption: *This is a market choice.*
Challenge: With 14 months of runway, it's really a question of what gets you to your next funding round or to profitability before the cash runs out.
Truth: Whichever option makes the next 9–10 months of metrics look strongest wins. You need to start fundraising with about 4–6 months of cash still in the bank, so your real window is closer to 8–10 months than 14.

Assumption: *Vietnam means growth.*
Challenge: A new country brings a new legal entity, licensing, payments, language, local hiring and go-to-market. Your burn rises right away, and revenue usually takes 6–12 months to show up. That's a large share of your remaining runway.
Truth: Expanding shortens your runway before it lengthens it.

Assumption: *Thailand is "known", so doubling down is safe.*
Challenge: If Thailand's growth is already flattening out, putting more money into it is also risky. It's just slower and harder to see.
Truth: The deciding fact is whether Thailand still returns more on each extra baht or dollar you spend. Right now that's unknown.

📍 **COUNCIL (where the voices disagree)**

- **Strategist:** If a competitor is about to lock up Vietnam, or investors will only fund a "regional" story, then being first can justify the burn.
- **Skeptic:** Most startups that die while expanding die from divided attention, not from picking the wrong market. A small team running two countries on 14 months of cash is the classic failure pattern.
- **Analyst:** Without your Thailand unit economics (CAC, payback, retention) and your Vietnam entry cost, the two options can't be compared. Today it's a story against a spreadsheet you haven't built yet.

📍 **OPTIONS AND SCENARIOS**

**Option A: Double down on Thailand**
- Trade-off: You keep burn under control and focus on proving repeatable growth, but you give up the regional story.
- What breaks it: Thailand is already saturating, or your investors require multi-market proof.

**Option B: Full Vietnam expansion next year**
- Trade-off: You get the regional narrative and a bigger addressable market, but burn rises and the team's focus is split during your fundraising window.
- What breaks it: Entry takes longer than about 6 months, or your Thailand metrics slip while leadership is distracted.

**Option C: Staged probe (the one that's usually overlooked)**
- Keep Thailand as the main focus. Test Vietnam cheaply with a partner or reseller, a few pilot customers, or remote sales, and don't set up a local entity.
- Cap the test at roughly 10–15% of burn, with a go/no-go date.
- Trade-off: The learning is slower and messier, but it keeps the Vietnam option open without betting the company.

I won't give you odds for these. Your inputs can't support numbers yet.

📍 **RECOMMENDATION**

My lean, conditionally, is **A with a small C attached**: make Thailand the engine and run Vietnam as a cheap, capped experiment. Go to full Vietnam expansion (B) only if both of these hold:
1. Thailand's marginal returns are clearly falling.
2. You have financing that pushes your runway past about 20 months, or an investor will fund the expansion specifically.

**First step:** Build a 14-month cash model for each option. For each one, mark the month you'd start fundraising and the metrics you'd be showing at that point. If Option B leaves you raising money with Vietnam still pre-revenue and Thailand flat, that settles it.

**Revisit:** At month 4, or right away if Thailand growth stalls two months in a row.
