The one hard fact you've given me is 14 months of runway, and that changes the question. You're not really choosing between Vietnam and Thailand. You're choosing which path gets you to profitability, or to your next round, before the cash runs out. A fundraise usually takes 4–6 months, so you have about 8–9 months to show results. That's less time than most country launches need to prove themselves.

**Five facts would settle this:**

1. **Is Thailand working?** Look at retention, how payback time on acquiring a customer is trending, and how much of the market you have. If growth is slowing because the market is saturated, that makes the case for Vietnam. If it's slowing because of product or execution problems, expanding just carries those problems into a second country.
2. **What would Vietnam cost?** Count the cash, and count the founder and team time pulled away from Thailand. Ask whether it would take 6 months or more of your runway.
3. **How much of the product carries over?** That means language, payments, regulation, distribution channels and local partners. More rebuilding means a slower and riskier launch.
4. **What do your investors need to see next round?** If they want depth in one market, Vietnam gets in the way. If they want proof you can win in more than one country, it helps.
5. **Do you have a real way into Vietnam?** A partner, a customer or a team member already there counts. "The market is big" doesn't.

**Two questions to answer first:**
- If you spent the Vietnam budget on Thailand, what result would that buy, and would it be enough to raise money?
- Could you test Vietnam cheaply, with a small pilot that has a set budget and a deadline for stopping, without committing to a full launch?

**One principle either way:** on a short runway, expansion should come from what's already working, not be used to escape what isn't. Your plan should not depend on both markets succeeding at once.
