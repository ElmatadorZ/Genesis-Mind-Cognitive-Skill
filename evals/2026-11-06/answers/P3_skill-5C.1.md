📍 FIRST PRINCIPLE BREAKDOWN

**Assumption:** "A new pricing plan" means a better pricing plan.
**Challenge:** Most pricing changes fix one problem and create another. A price rise lifts margin but can raise churn. A free tier brings in signups but can eat into paid conversion.
**Truth:** A pricing plan is only good in relation to a specific goal. If no goal is written down, it can't be judged.

**Assumption:** Customers will see the new pricing the way you intend.
**Challenge:** Customers compare against what they paid before and against competitors, not against your internal logic.
**Truth:** The key reaction is how existing customers respond to the change itself, not to the new price in isolation.

**Assumption:** The plan's success can be measured.
**Challenge:** Without a baseline and a test period, you won't be able to tell whether the plan worked or the market just moved.
**Truth:** A plan without success metrics agreed in advance is a guess, even if the guess turns out right.

📍 MULTI-AGENT INSIGHT

**Analyst:** There's no data to analyse yet. The minimum needed is old vs. new prices, revenue and customer count by segment, and unit cost.
**Strategist:** The best pricing changes match price to the value each segment actually gets. Ask who pays more under the new plan, who pays less, and whether that matches who gets the most value.
**Skeptic:** The most common failure is a plan designed from the inside (cost or revenue targets) instead of from how customers actually buy. The second is surprising existing customers.
**Forecaster:** See the scenarios below. These are generic shapes, not predictions about your plan.
**Executor:** Don't launch everything at once. Test the plan on new customers first, or on one segment, and set the pass/fail metrics before launch.

📍 SCENARIOS (structural, not plan-specific)

🐂 **Bull:** Price matches value, existing customers are handled well, and margin rises with little churn.
🐻 **Bear:** The change feels like a price grab, loyal customers leave or complain publicly, and the gain in revenue per customer doesn't make up for the customers lost.
⚖️ **Base:** A mixed result. One segment improves and another shrinks, and the net effect depends on segment mix. That's why segment data matters.
💀 **Black Swan:** A competitor undercuts you right as you launch, or the change triggers a reaction that damages your brand beyond pricing.

---

📍 DECISION OPTIONS

**Option A: Full rollout to everyone**
Trade-off: fastest impact and simplest to run.
Risk: no way to separate cause from noise, and the most exposure to backlash from existing customers.

**Option B: Staged rollout (new customers first, existing customers grandfathered or given notice)**
Trade-off: slower to show results, and you run two price lists for a while.
Risk: lower, because you learn before you commit.

**Option C: Hold and validate first (customer interviews, a competitor check, unit-economics review)**
Trade-off: delays the launch.
Risk: the lowest, unless timing really matters.
