**The factors that most often decide whether an AI startup survives**

1. **Thin wrapper, no moat.** The product is a prompt and a UI sitting on someone else's model. When the model vendor ships the same feature, or a better base model makes the workaround pointless, the value is gone.
2. **Inference costs eat the margin.** Every user action costs compute. If pricing doesn't cover token costs at scale, more growth means bigger losses.
3. **Demo-to-production gap.** A good demo isn't 95%+ reliability in a real workflow. Many teams find out that last stretch (evals, edge cases, integrations, compliance) is most of the work.
4. **A solution looking for a problem.** "We use AI" is a capability, not a buyer's pain. Pilots get signed out of curiosity and then don't renew.
5. **Capital timing.** Rounds raised on hype set valuations that later traction can't justify, so the next raise fails before the product matures.

**Questions to ask first**
- Fail compared to what: other software startups of the same age, or startups in general?
- Which kind of AI startup: application-layer wrappers, vertical tools, or model and infrastructure companies? Their failure modes are very different.

**Epistemic Core**

ASSUMPTION: That "most AI startups fail within two years" is an established fact. I don't have a solid source for that rate **[UNVERIFIED]**, and young startups in most sectors fail at high rates, so AI may not be unusual.

KNOWN / UNKNOWN: The failure modes above are inferred from the structure of the market. The real failure rate, and how it splits by layer, is unknown.

COUNTER: Maybe AI startups fail for the usual reasons (no product-market fit, running out of cash, team problems), and "AI" adds little explanation beyond faster hype cycles.

CONFIDENCE: Moderate on the mechanisms. The size of the failure rate can't be assessed yet without cohort data.

WHAT CHANGES THIS: Cohort data showing AI survival rates in line with other software startups, or showing that infrastructure and vertical players survive far longer than wrappers.
