If you paste in the details, I'll give you a straight verdict. These are the things I'd check:

1. **What changes, and for whom.** Compare old and new prices side by side for each customer type. Who pays more, who pays less, and who has to switch plans?
2. **Margin per unit.** For each tier, does the price still cover your real costs with room to spare? That means product, shipping, platform fees, and your time. A plan that grows revenue but shrinks margin is a trap.
3. **Your best customers.** Look at the top 20% who bring in most of your revenue. If the new plan raises their price or makes things harder for them, that's the biggest risk.
4. **Clarity.** Can a new customer pick the right option in about 10 seconds? Too many tiers or confusing add-ons usually lose more sales than a higher price does.
5. **Anchoring and the middle option.** Is there a clear "most people pick this" choice, priced so the options around it make it look like good value?
6. **Market reality.** Is it priced sensibly against the alternatives your customers are actually comparing you to?
7. **The break-even question.** How many customers could you lose before the new plan earns less than the old one? If that number is small, the plan is fragile.
8. **Rollout.** Will existing customers keep their old price (grandfathering) or be moved over? Have you planned how to announce it, and can you test it on new customers first?
